## 1. Locked

- **Start date.** The chart begins ~3200-3000 BCE. Slice 1 is 3200-2800 BCE.

## 2. The rules

**Inclusion criteria.** One test: a group gets a stream if it holds, or seriously contests, effective political control over a meaningful share of the relevant world at that time, as a settled state, a city-network, or a nomadic/pastoral confederation.
- Writing is not a requirement.
- Require systemic complexity beyond a single village: specialised production, long-distance exchange, surplus extraction, or a multi-settlement hierarchy.
- The unit is a cultural-political bloc, not an ethnic label.

**Grouping rule.** City-states count as one bloc. Sumer is one stream in 3200-2800 BCE, not Uruk vs Ur vs Lagash. Split only when one polity unifies more than half the bloc, or imposes a single administration.

**Residual rule.** Explicit "Other" streams exist from day one so widths sum to 100%. For Slice 1: "Other settled farming peoples" and "Other pastoralist / mobile peoples". The residual will dominate early slices, the honest correction to the original. No mythical entities, no purely linguistic groups with no polity.

**Scope.** Global: all qualifying societies everywhere. A group gets its own stream only by passing the test; everything else falls into the residuals.

**Width metric (F3, locked 2026-11-26).** One metric: controlled productive land weighted by estimated agricultural productivity. A single land-based measure that also tracks population. Population is recorded per stream in the notes as a cross-check, never mixed into the width. Raw area is the fallback where productivity data is missing.

**Measurement conventions.** The denominator expands over time (100% in 3200 BCE is much smaller than in 1500 CE). Estimates are ranges, not false precision. Contested land is counted once, assigned to the dominant controller at that date, with the contest noted.
