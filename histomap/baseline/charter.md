# Histomap Rebuild - Baseline Charter

**Version:** 0.1 (eesel's proposal)
**Date:** 2026-11-26
**Status:** NOT YET ALIGNED. Four-party sign-off pending (repo owner, eesel, Gemini, Grok).

## Purpose

Rebuild John B. Sparks' Histomap from the ground up: a fixed-width chart where width = share of the relevant world, each civilization or cultural-political bloc drawn as a stream, built up in chronological slices. Goal is a more rigorous and globally complete version than the 1931 original.

## 1. Inclusion criteria

**eesel's position: MODIFY.**

Keep a threshold, but replace the four-part AND-checklist with a single power test. The chart measures political power sharing a fixed width, so the gate should be about power, not a culture checklist.

**The test:** A group gets a stream if it *holds, or seriously contests, effective political control over a meaningful share of the relevant world at that time.*

The four proposed bullets become *evidence for* the test rather than gates:

- Durable settlements - evidence of sustained population concentration (not required).
- Centralized or semi-centralized authority - required, but chiefdoms, confederations and tribute networks count.
- Organizational complexity - monumental architecture, hierarchy, tribute extraction, military mobilization.
- Reach beyond a local chiefdom - required.

**Change from the proposal: drop writing as a criterion.** Writing is a regional accident (Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, Mesoamerica), not a measure of power. Keeping it as a gate would erase the Andes (Caral / Norte Chico, monumental, non-literate) and later exclude steppe confederations (Xiongnu, Huns, the early Mongol phase) that dominated enormous territories. "Administrative complexity" already captures what we actually mean.

**Open question raised by eesel - the non-state majority.** At 3000 BCE almost everyone lived outside any qualifying polity. If width = 100% of the relevant land, those people need a representation. Options: (a) a thin neutral "other / non-state / unattested" band, or (b) redefine the whole width as *state-controlled* territory. A chart that silently fills 100% with states is misrepresenting the world. We must pick one.

## 2. What the full width represents

**eesel's position: AGREE IN SPIRIT, DENOMINATOR MUST BE DEFINED.**

Width = share of the relevant world. But "relevant world" and the metric both need pinning down.

- **Primary meaning:** share of the relevant world's *population*. Power tracks people and the surplus they generate better than raw land area.
- **Estimator:** area x plausible density, so an empty steppe or desert does not count the same as irrigated Mesopotamia.
- **Scope ("the relevant world"):** the set of regions in political/economic contact ("in play") at that time. This scope grows over the centuries.

**Trap to name up front:** as the scope grows, a civilization of constant absolute size *narrows* on the chart. Readers will misread that as decline. We need a convention - draw scope growth explicitly with an edge marker, or normalize widths.

**Big fork the team must decide:** does "globally complete" mean

- (a) the *connected* world, growing over time (this is what the original did), or
- (b) *all* qualifying societies everywhere, including the Americas and sub-Saharan Africa from antiquity?

Recommend (b) as the target, while noting the heavy data cost.

## 3. Starting date and slicing

**eesel's position: AGREE ON ORIGIN, COUNTER ON FIRST SLICE.**

- Agree the chart origin at ~3200-3000 BCE is right (it is roughly why the original starts at 3000).
- **Counter-propose the first working slice as 3500-3000 BCE**, to catch the Uruk expansion, late Predynastic Egypt, and the Caral phenomenon together.
- **Reason:** our own criterion admits non-literate monumental societies, and Caral (~3500 BCE) is one. Starting at 3200 while excluding Caral *by date* contradicts our own rule. If the team keeps 3200-2800, exclude Caral on *scope* grounds instead ("outside the contact sphere of the time") and state that decision in this charter.
- **Slicing should be uneven:** coarse in the deep past, finer near the present. Uniform slices would need absurd numbers of rows later. See `../collaboration/workflow.md` for the proposed scheme.

## 4. Conventions the current proposal does not yet cover

1. **Non-state majority band** (see point 1).
2. **Contestation and overlap** - nomads, imperial frontiers, tributary zones. Allow overlapping streams (with hatching/shading) or force exclusive streams?
3. **Scope growth / the false-decline trap** (see point 2).
4. **Naming convention** - civilization vs polity. E.g. a stable "Mesopotamia" stream with polity sub-labels (Akkad, Ur III, Babylon), rather than a new stream per dynasty.
5. **Sources and confidence** - cite ranges and mark low-confidence widths so the chart is auditable.

## 5. What each party is asked to do

Reply with **agree / modify / counter-propose** on points 1-3, and take a position on the point 4 forks. Once aligned, this file becomes v1 and we start the first slice.
