# Slice 1: 3200-2800 BCE

**Status:** DRAFT. Revised after Gemini's review; Grok's review is logged below and diverges on the named-state levels; Muse's review is pending. Metric locked: controlled productive land weighted by estimated agricultural productivity; population as a cross-check; raw area as the fallback. Every figure is a reasoned order-of-magnitude estimate given as a range, not a measurement.

## Scope (regions in play this era)

The connected complex-society world of the late 4th millennium BCE: southern Mesopotamia, the Nile valley, the Iranian plateau fringe, and the Indus basin. Other regions with emerging complexity are tracked inside the residuals (see below) rather than as standalone streams.

## The live divergence: how big are the named states

Gemini and Grok agree on the approach and on the residuals, but split on the named-state levels, and on Sumer in opposite directions:

- **Gemini:** nudge Sumer up to **4-7%** (the irrigated alluvium was at least on par with the Nile). Named states ~13-19%.
- **Grok:** tighten Sumer down to **2.5-5%**, Egypt 3.5-6.5%, Elam 1-2.5%, Early Harappan 2-5%; named states ~10-16%. Grok puts Sumer's controlled high-productivity alluvium at only ~10,000-20,000 km2.

The crux is a **land-area question**: how much high-productivity irrigable land each core actually controlled in 3200-2800 BCE. The draft's Sumer figure (40,000-60,000 km2) sits between the two reviewers and needs a shared source before we lock. Egypt's cultivable area is put at ~15,000-20,000 km2 by Grok (Butzer-style) versus 20,000-35,000 in the draft.

**Resolution path:** pick one citable land-area basis for Slice 1, apply the same productivity weights to all streams, and let the numbers fall out. Muse's review may break the tie.

## Decisions confirmed by both reviewers

- **Pastoral weighting floor kept at 0.2** (Gemini and Grok agree): mobile peoples controlled and contested vast zones; weighting them to zero would break the control/contestation rule and distort later steppe streams. Grok suggests 0.2-0.25 is the right band.
- **Caral-Supe and the Yellow River (Late Yangshao / Early Longshan) stay inside the settled residual for Slice 1**, documented below, and break out as standalone streams later (Gemini: Slice 2, 2800-2400 BCE).
- **Proto-Elamite / Elam stay combined** as one stream in Slice 1.
- **Rendering:** residuals on the outer flanks in muted tones, named states in the centre in saturated colours.

## Residual notes

The "Other settled farming peoples" residual dominates this slice (roughly half the width). It documents why two emerging centres stay inside the residual here rather than breaking out as streams, so the inclusion bar is applied consistently across the world and the slice is not read as Old World bias:

- **Caral-Supe (Norte Chico, Peru):** monumental construction at Huaricanga and Caral begins around 3100-3000 BCE, so it covers only the late part of this window and lacks broad regional hegemony yet. Stays in the residual for Slice 1; breaks out in Slice 2.
- **Late Yangshao / Early Longshan (Yellow River basin):** competing walled towns and chiefdoms (Central Plains, Dawenkou/Shandong), not yet a unified bloc or urban state network. Stays in the residual for Slice 1; breaks out in Slice 2.
