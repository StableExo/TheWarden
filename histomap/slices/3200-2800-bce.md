# Slice 1: 3200-2800 BCE

**Status:** FIRST DRAFT of the width ranges (Claude, 2026-11-26), posted for the team to attack. Metric locked: controlled productive land weighted by estimated agricultural productivity; population as a cross-check in the notes; raw area as the fallback. Every figure below is a reasoned order-of-magnitude estimate given as a range, not a measurement.

## Scope (regions in play this era)

The connected complex-society world of the late 4th millennium BCE: southern Mesopotamia, the Nile valley, the Iranian plateau fringe, and the Indus basin. Other regions with emerging complexity are tracked inside the residuals (see below) rather than as standalone streams.

**Combined:** the four named states total roughly **12-18%**; the two residuals roughly **82-88%**.

## How these were derived (Claude's reasoning)

- The denominator is all controlled or contested productive land within the global scope (the four states plus both residuals).
- The four named states sit on the most productive land (Nile, Tigris-Euphrates, Indus), so weighting by productivity lifts them above what raw area alone would give.
- Even so, they control only a small part of the world's farmed land in 3000 BCE, so the residual dominates. That is the honest correction to the original Histomap.
- Cross-check with population: world population at 3000 BCE is often estimated at roughly 14-20M. The four states plausibly held about 2-4M between them, i.e. about 12-25%, which brackets the 12-18% width estimate.

## Residual notes

The "Other settled farming peoples" residual is expected to dominate this slice (roughly half the width). It should document why two emerging centres stay inside the residual here rather than breaking out as streams, so the inclusion bar is applied consistently across the world and the slice is not read as Old World bias:

- **Caral-Supe (Norte Chico, Peru):** monumental complexes emerging around 3000 BCE, so they fall late inside this window. Tracked in the residual for Slice 1; a candidate to break out once it clearly passes the full test in a later slice.
- **Late Yangshao / Early Longshan (Yellow River basin):** laying the urban groundwork, not yet clearly past the test this slice. Tracked in the residual.

## Open questions

- **Pastoralist weighting.** Productivity weighting pushes the mobile peoples down even though they controlled vast territory, so a floor weight (0.2) was applied. If we let productivity weight them out, the settled residual grows and the pastoral stream shrinks. This is the single judgement call that moves the slice most.
- Caral and Longshan: qualify this slice, or stay in the residual?
- Sumer and Elam overlap: is the Proto-Elamite horizon best treated as one stream or split from Elam proper?
